# Fortis Delivery Methodology — Internal Reference Notes

These are working notes for internal use. Not polished for external distribution. Dan last updated Sept 2023. If using for a bid please rewrite into proper format and get approval.

---

Our approach to penetration testing engagements generally follows PTES (Penetration Testing Execution Standard) as the structural backbone with OWASP for web app work and we layer in our own internal quality requirements on top. The seven phases of PTES (pre-engagement interactions, intelligence gathering, threat modelling, vulnerability analysis, exploitation, post-exploitation, reporting) give us a reproducible structure that senior reviewers can check against. For CHECK engagements we must also comply with the HMG IA Standard No 1 Technical Risk Assessment guidance and the CHECK scheme requirements document as published by NCSC - these take precedence over our own methodology where there is any conflict.

Pre-engagement is where we do the most important work that clients often don't see. This includes agreeing the Rules of Engagement (RoE) document (which defines what is in scope, out of scope, what constitutes a halt condition, emergency contacts, and the test window). We never start active testing without a signed RoE and a signed Statement of Work. This is non-negotiable and has protected us legally on two occasions where clients tried to claim we had caused damage to systems that were actually already broken before we started. The SoW and RoE are the first things we check if there is any dispute. Get legal to review if the client tries to modify the standard RoE template significantly - Ben is the first call on this.

Intelligence gathering varies by engagement type. For external assessments OSINT is a core component: we use a combination of Shodan, Censys, WHOIS historical data (DomainTools where we have budget), Certificate Transparency logs (crt.sh), LinkedIn for employee enumeration (useful for spear phishing pretext), Google dorking, theHarvester, and Maltego for relationship mapping. For internal assessments OSINT is often less relevant but can still inform the threat model. We document all OSINT findings in an Intelligence Report section even if they don't lead to exploitable paths, because clients find it valuable to know what an attacker can see from outside.

Vulnerability analysis uses a combination of automated scanning and manual review. We do NOT rely solely on automated tools. The scanning stack typically includes: Nessus Professional for authenticated network scans (we have a team licence), Nmap for discovery and service fingerprinting, Nikto/OWASP ZAP for web app initial crawl, Burp Suite Professional for web app manual testing (all testers have a licence), BloodHound/SharpHound for AD enumeration on internal engagements, Responder for LLMNR/NBT-NS poisoning on internal networks (with client consent documented in RoE). For OT engagements we use Claroty SRA (when available via client) or passive network capture with Wireshark and analysis of S7comm/Modbus/DNP3 traffic. We do not run active scanners in OT environments without explicit written approval from the OT system owner - this is a hard rule following an incident at a client site in 2021 where an automated scan caused a PLC to enter fault state.

Exploitation follows the vulnerability analysis phase. We always aim to demonstrate exploitability rather than just flag theoretical vulnerabilities - clients get more value from "we ran this code and got a SYSTEM shell on your domain controller" than from "CVE-2023-XXXX applies to your Windows version." Where exploitation could cause service disruption we agree the test window with the client in advance (usually during change management hours). Post-exploitation covers privilege escalation, lateral movement, persistence demonstration (not actual persistence - we don't leave backdoors), and data exfiltration simulation (typically we copy a test file to a staging server to demonstrate the capability without exfiltrating real data). All post-exploitation activity is logged with timestamps.

Reporting is structured as: Executive Summary (2-3 pages, board-suitable, no technical jargon, risk-rated summary, top 3 recommended actions) + Technical Report (full findings with CVE references, CVSS v3.1 scores, reproduction steps, screenshots/output, and remediation recommendations). Findings are rated Critical / High / Medium / Low / Informational using CVSS base score as the starting point but adjusted for context (a CVSS 9.8 vuln that requires physical access to exploit may be reduced; a CVSS 5.0 vuln that leads directly to Crown Jewels may be elevated). All reports go through a QA review by the Technical Director (Dan) before delivery. Reports go out as PDF only - we don't send Word/editable versions as standard (clients can request this but we note it in the engagement log).

For CE and CE+ our delivery process is documented separately in the IASME Assessor Operations Manual but in brief: scoping call to agree system boundary (this is the most contentious part - clients often want to narrow scope to exclude awkward systems and we have to balance reasonable boundary-setting with scope gaming), self-assessment questionnaire support if needed, verification (CE+ only - involves authenticated vulnerability scan using Tenable.io, firewall rule review, configuration sampling of 10% of device types or 5 devices minimum whichever is greater), findings discussion, re-verification if needed (usually within 30 days at no additional cost for one re-test), certificate issuance via IASME portal, findings report for CE+ whether passed or failed. We aim for a turnaround of 8 working days from completed submission to certificate assuming no remediation is required. If we identify critical findings during CE+ verification we notify the client same-day verbally and follow up in writing within 24hrs even if the full report is not ready.

Incident response engagements follow our IR playbook (see separate document IR-Playbook-v3) but the key principles that differ from pen testing are: evidence preservation is the top priority before any remediation action (image first, clean later); maintain chain of custody for any forensic artefacts (date/time stamps, SHA-256 hashes of images, documented handling log); assume the attacker may still be present until proven otherwise and treat containment as urgent; communicate only on out-of-band channels if there is any possibility the attacker is monitoring internal systems (use personal mobile phones for voice, avoid corporate email until the email system has been assessed); involve legal counsel early especially if personal data is involved (UK GDPR 72hr ICO notification clock starts from when the organisation became "aware" of a breach - help the client understand when this clock starts); and document everything with timestamps throughout.

On the governance side, our vCISO methodology is built around the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover) as the primary reference, with ISO 27001 Annex A controls as the control library. We produce a Risk Register for each vCISO client in our standard format (risk ID, threat description, vulnerability, likelihood 1-5, impact 1-5, inherent risk score, current controls, residual risk score, treatment, owner, review date). Risk register is reviewed monthly with the client and updated quarterly as a minimum. Board reporting follows a traffic light format (RAG status per domain) that we've found works well for non-technical boards. We also maintain a Controls Register linked to the Risk Register.

Quality assurance points: no report leaves without QA by a senior reviewer (Technical Director for pen test, Head of Governance for compliance reports), all working papers are stored in the client folder on our SharePoint (ISO 27001 document control requirements - folder structure defined), time recording is mandatory for all billable and non-billable work (we use Harvest), client satisfaction surveys are sent at close of each engagement (scores tracked by ops). We track re-test findings closure rates - clients who close >80% of medium+ findings within 90 days get a note in their account record for bid reference purposes.
